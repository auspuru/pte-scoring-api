# SWT pre-revision passage and reference review

This appendix covers the ten original advanced passages stored in the revision file, the 20 standalone key/sample pairs in the repository immediately before the sample revision, and the five sample-only replacements whose earlier source text the patch does not identify. The exact pairs were recovered from `1435f49:content/swt-advanced.json` and `2ae4441:passages.json`. The older versions add **12 distinct texts and 26 exact reference variants** beyond the current audit. The five sample-only replacements have incomplete historical source provenance and are recorded separately.

The 12 older texts below have either been replaced or corrected. Current portal and frozen-paper coverage is recorded separately in the [passage audit](swt-passage-audit.md). The shorter archived versions receive their own interpretation, particularly when later wording added a relationship or condition.

### The Limits of Measurement

**Main point.** Indicators help evaluate institutions but can divert work toward measured outcomes, so human judgement and revisable measures are essential.

**Essential meaning.** Keep the incentive to favour counted activities and the tension between useful metrics and unchecked judgement. Qualitative evidence and revision address this.

**Optional.** Examples of exams, waiting times and publication counts.

**Source evidence.** “when behaviour adapts to them”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample accurately keep the risk and response. The later version broadens the problem from incentives to categories that can exclude institutional responsibilities.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `0cadff6c797b27641936ae86c1e2044d765f96dc4e576565d56f0d2258af77f9`

### Ecological Restoration and Historical Baselines

**Main point.** Restoration should build durable ecological capacity rather than copy a disputed historical baseline.

**Essential meaning.** History constrains choices, climate and ecological change affect feasibility, and new arrangements can help without excusing losses. State ecological or cultural aims and long-term viability.

**Optional.** Specific causes of landscape change or examples of novel species combinations.

**Source evidence.** “between historical fidelity and future viability”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample match the debate and the forward-looking aim. The later version adds accountability for what is given up, retained and made possible.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `80f6b9247dea049b4ebb73df014330a1feb20f42d297ca01f2c938b1b0e8388d`

### Expertise and Democratic Legitimacy

**Main point.** Legitimate decisions combine credible expertise with public scrutiny of values, risks and burdens.

**Essential meaning.** Experts assess likely effects and reveal assumptions. Affected people deliberate about acceptable trade-offs while applying evidence standards.

**Optional.** Particular timescales, examples of risks and decision procedures.

**Source evidence.** “the two address different, although interdependent, questions”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample preserve complementary responsibilities. The later version sharpens the issue of allocation and introduces neglected experience correcting knowledge.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `f2b1fd25b968aa2f3951f45d8a6d8f0dd5f3ba57eff291808a9d25cebdfbecc1`

### The Paradox of Organisational Memory

**Main point.** Routines preserve past lessons but may outlive them, so organisations should understand and test them before reform.

**Essential meaning.** Coordination makes routines valuable and difficult to change. Removing them without knowing their purpose can reopen failures, so investigate which protections remain necessary.

**Optional.** Manuals, personnel departures, technologies and types of organisational defender.

**Source evidence.** “may recreate vulnerabilities”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample support selective retention and revision. Later examples of trials and recorded exceptions do not belong to this version.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `0156fe8392a06e633df923fdc833ecd46b38bec429bc3680c02198a97922445f`

### Translation as Intellectual Negotiation

**Main point.** Translation should communicate culturally embedded meaning while keeping important differences visible.

**Essential meaning.** Literal rendering can lose practical force, and familiar wording can erase cultural difference. Evidence, context and purpose constrain a translator's choices.

**Optional.** Legal, politeness and irony examples.

**Source evidence.** “Choices remain answerable to textual evidence”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample keep both practical force and responsible choices. A footnote and deniability example appear only in the later version.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `b07bc6b9c3f1fe6f3f1942d1898c8786ac458eef51a5a36449c4caac9158c6c7`

### Urban Efficiency and Redundancy

**Main point.** A city's spare capacity can protect it during disruption, when practical access matters.

**Essential meaning.** Maximising routine utilisation can remove useful reserves. Assess costs and distribute reserves so vulnerable residents can use them in a crisis.

**Optional.** Specific vacancies, routes or facilities.

**Source evidence.** “distinguish waste from strategically distributed flexibility”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample keep the efficiency and resilience trade-off. Shared failure points and operational independence are explicit only in the later version.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `4329e31e2b3bb6a7f097cc413bb61c53ad9227325b340cd1541ebab9e5102d6f`

### Scientific Models and Productive Simplification

**Main point.** Simplification helps models explain, provided omissions suit the question and assumptions are tested.

**Essential meaning.** Context affects transferability. Specify limits and test sensitivity and external evidence rather than assuming mathematical precision guarantees suitability.

**Optional.** Examples of broad versus local inference, kinds of modelling technique.

**Source evidence.** “appropriate to the inquiry”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample preserve purpose and validation. The later version adds different mechanisms producing the same calibration fit.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `ee4b2a5545d877f67d0c465036c7725190cb2058291315e9dafc4c13fe3e1c86`

### The Distribution of Attention

**Main point.** Ranking systems shape public attention when information is abundant, so their power should be transparent and open to challenge.

**Essential meaning.** Engagement can amplify reactive content and steer what producers make. An authoritative quality standard also concentrates power. Meaningful choice and diverse intermediaries help.

**Optional.** Outrage and misunderstanding examples, particular user controls.

**Source evidence.** “Individual choices and automated selection reinforce one another”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample explain how attention is shaped and governed. The later version makes the visibility and engagement loop more explicit and stresses sustaining long-term value.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `d510f53d688b8afeb02b731e498a64108c89e6ab353cec1dae2ca683459046fa`

### Intergenerational Stewardship

**Main point.** People making decisions now should preserve future choices under uncertain preferences.

**Essential meaning.** Future people cannot participate. Avoid irreversible harm and large transferred burdens while keeping institutions adaptable and considering present needs.

**Optional.** Infrastructure, debt and future technologies.

**Source evidence.** “Their absence from current debate”

**Reference review.** Distinct stakes and choices are clear in the keys and sample. Detailed discounting and burdens on the present poor are elaborated only in the later version.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `605dc741f80481235e94e2d9a309e595754da378ab470e1d0a40a88b4d141d40`

### Tacit Knowledge and Automation

**Main point.** Automation should support judgement and preserve the experience needed for human oversight.

**Essential meaning.** Rules miss some contextual judgements. Practice develops expertise, which can carry biases. Machines can reveal patterns while people recognise exceptions, but they need learning opportunities.

**Optional.** Technician and teacher examples, specific patterns and practitioner communities.

**Source evidence.** “If automation removes all occasions for learning”

**Reference review.** Keys and sample correctly describe the joint roles and the learning dependency. The later version adds the loop in which deference suppresses documented errors.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `1a8b5c133a078f9cd3f9c5b3ff64899f21f3abac356771278f34880b58471469`

### Climate Change Economics

**Main point.** Climate change threatens significant and unequal losses, and falling renewable costs make transition possible if investment scales.

**Essential meaning.** Connect the mismatch between developing nations' historic emissions and losses. Preserve the investment condition for responding, with source claims appropriately attributed.

**Optional.** Numerical projections, names, individual sectors and the later-removed military comparison.

**Source evidence.** “requires $4 trillion in annual investment”

**Reference review.** The earlier key and sample repeated a military-spending comparison that the subsequent source correction removed. The sample also presents the historical emissions percentage without its timeframe. Use the revised sample, which keeps the cost and equity relationships without repeating the removed comparison.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `19fc4ba3bc2c6eb607d35a2560cf1086428f0cccf87103a130e5b271fa4e4c8c`

### The New Woman

**Main point.** The New Woman challenged limits on women's roles and influenced later reforms despite resistance.

**Essential meaning.** Keep the debate about women's employment, financial freedom or marriage, the mixed response and historically lasting effect. The old damaged phrase about realisation is recoverable from the contrast.

**Optional.** Exact dates, named regions and the details of the book's reception.

**Source evidence.** “laid the groundwork for future reforms”

**Reference review.** The old key and sample capture influence, but the sample is excerpt-heavy. The later source restores “realised” in the damaged clause. No student's response should need to reproduce the damaged wording.

**Exact text SHA-256.** `61faddd49a49871f47ead468ceb4aae62185a1b24ba03fb1aaa75b8b815f7f3e`
