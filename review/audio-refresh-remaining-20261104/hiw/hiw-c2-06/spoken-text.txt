Inviting people to speak does not by itself ensure that their contributions receive equitable consideration. A participant may be formally included while the credibility of their testimony is discounted through assumptions about expertise or social standing. Conversely, familiar institutional language can confer authority on a claim whose evidential basis remains weak. Addressing this asymmetry requires more than an indiscriminate declaration that every statement deserves equal weight. It requires examining how credibility is allocated, what opportunities exist to challenge an interpretation, and whether participants possess the resources to make their experiences intelligible within the discussion. Deliberation improves when these conditions become explicit and revisable. The aim is neither automatic deference nor reflexive scepticism, but a process in which the reasons for accepting or rejecting a contribution can themselves be questioned.
