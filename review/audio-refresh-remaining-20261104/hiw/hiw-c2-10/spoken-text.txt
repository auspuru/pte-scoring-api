A public programme may satisfy its numerical targets while failing to realise the purpose for which those targets were introduced. Once an indicator becomes consequential for funding, organisations have an incentive to optimise what is counted, even when doing so displaces less measurable responsibilities. This divergence is not an inevitable consequence of quantification, but it reveals the danger of treating a proxy as an exhaustive definition of success. Evaluation should examine how reported improvements were produced and whether their benefits endure beyond the reporting interval. Independent scrutiny and qualitative evidence can expose achievements that are nominal rather than substantive. A credible assessment preserves the usefulness of measurement while keeping its categories subordinate to the public purposes they are meant to illuminate, rather than allowing the categories themselves to dictate those purposes.
