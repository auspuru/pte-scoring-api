Reading a familiar page can create a strong impression that its contents have been learned. However, recognition while the information remains visible is different from retrieving it without assistance. Students who close the book and attempt to explain an idea may discover gaps that repeated reading had concealed. These difficulties can be useful because they identify what needs further attention. Effective retrieval practice includes feedback, allowing mistaken answers to be corrected before they become established. It also benefits from spacing, with material revisited after an interval instead of rehearsed continuously in a single sitting. The aim is not to make every practice attempt effortless. Some manageable difficulty can strengthen later recall, provided that the learner eventually reaches an accurate understanding and connects individual facts to a coherent explanation.
