Washing fresh produce can remove soil and reduce some surface contamination before food is eaten or prepared. Running water is usually appropriate, while firm produce can be cleaned with a brush that is kept for food use. Pre-washed packaged greens may not need another wash if the label says they are ready to eat, because extra handling can introduce contamination. Cleaning the sink, hands and utensils is just as important as rinsing the food itself. Good produce hygiene is therefore a simple routine that combines clean water, clean equipment and attention to package instructions.
